# M11 — Privacy in Pattern Classification

This module examines how useful patterns can expose people, groups, and
sensitive attributes. It connects concrete privacy compromises to the
neighbouring-dataset idea and the controlled use of randomness in differential
privacy.

## Learning outcomes

After completing this module, learners should be able to:

1. identify disclosure, linkage, re-identification, and inference risks in a
   pattern-classification workflow;
2. explain why removing direct identifiers does not by itself guarantee
   privacy;
3. state the intuition of neighbouring datasets and a differential-privacy
   guarantee; and
4. reason about privacy budget, sensitivity, utility, and responsible
   communication of results.

## Topic sequence

- behavioural traces, data linkage, uniqueness, and routes to privacy
  compromise;
- the limits of anonymisation and aggregation;
- query sensitivity and neighbouring datasets;
- randomised response, noise mechanisms, privacy budget, composition, and the
  privacy-utility trade-off;
- provenance, consent, fairness, retention, and limitations in a complete
  classification workflow.
